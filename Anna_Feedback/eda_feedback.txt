Normality in the summary statistics

In the passage on total consumption, you say similar means and medians do not establish normality. Whether something is Gaussian comes up later, in the assumption checks. The readers who care about normality do not need to hear it at the descriptive stage. This sentence can be cut.

Unclear passage on Pearson correlation

I do not understand what your passage on normal-theory inference for Pearson correlation means. You say beverage consumption is concentrated at low values with a long right tail, concentration scores are discrete and bounded from 0 to 10, and these features warrant caution. Say directly which assumption you are worried about and why these features matter for it.

Independence

You did not need to comment on independence. We can assume students are not collaborating to fill out a questionnaire, and there is no reason to suspect otherwise. The paragraph on independence and sampling adds nothing.

Adjusted R^2

You say a negative adjusted $R^2$ indicates that the improvement in fit does not compensate for the number of predictors. I do not think it necessarily means that. Adjusted $R^2$ penalizes each predictor you add, and it is mainly used for model selection. I would leave it out of your report, where it is just noise for this study.

Consistent wording

You need to be careful about wording. In the passage about removing the 20-serving response, you refer to the adjusted beverage estimate. The variable is called "Caffeinated drinks (per serving/day)." Beverages are similar, but not all beverages contain caffeine, so use the variable name consistently.

Statements you can cut

Your audience knows statistics, so you do not need to say that a nonsignificant test does not prove no relationship, or that observational self-reports over seven days do not support causal claims. These statements can be cut.

Unclear closing passage

I do not understand what you are getting at in the passage that starts with "Equal beverage volumes can contain different caffeine doses." It lists several limitations without connecting them to your results, and the closing suggestion about checking high consumption against original responses is hard to follow.
